Complete User Guide to Solving Quadratic Equations, Discriminants, and Parabola Vertices

Why Quadratic Equations Appear Across Algebra, Physics, and Engineering

A quadratic equation in standard form ax² + bx + c = 0 (where a ≠ 0) models second-degree relationships and parabolic curves across mathematics, projectile motion, optimization problems, area geometry, and circuit analysis. Finding the roots—the values of x where the quadratic expression equals zero—can be done by factoring, completing the square, or applying the quadratic formula, while the discriminant immediately tells you whether the roots are real or complex. A browser-based Quadratic Equation Solver computes the roots, discriminant, vertex coordinates, axis of symmetry, and parabola opening direction automatically—100% locally in your browser with zero server uploads.

How to Solve a Quadratic Equation in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Write the Equation in Standard Form: Arrange terms so the equation equals zero in the form ax² + bx + c = 0, keeping negative signs attached to b or c.
  2. Enter Coefficients a, b, and c: Type numerical values into the a, b, and c fields (with a non-zero), or click Load Sample (x² − 5x + 6 = 0).
  3. Inspect Roots & Parabola Properties: Read the roots, discriminant (Δ), vertex (h, k), and axis of symmetry, then copy or download a plain-text solution summary.

Quadratic Formula, Discriminant & Vertex Formulas

Using discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac, the quadratic formula and vertex coordinates are:

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a)  |  Δ = b² − 4ac
Vertex: h = −b / (2a),   k = a(h)² + b(h) + c

Understanding Discriminant Cases & Edge Inputs

  • Positive Discriminant (Δ > 0): Produces two distinct real roots and two separate x-intercepts on the parabola.
  • Zero Discriminant (Δ = 0): Produces one repeated real root (a double root) where the parabola's vertex touches the x-axis.
  • Negative Discriminant (Δ < 0): Produces two complex conjugate roots involving i = √(−1), with no real x-intercepts.

Worked Example: For x² − 5x + 6 = 0 (a = 1, b = −5, c = 6), Δ = (−5)² − 4(1)(6) = 1, giving real roots x₁ = 3 and x₂ = 2, vertex (2.5, −0.25), and axis of symmetry x = 2.5.
