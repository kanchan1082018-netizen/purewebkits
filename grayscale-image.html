How ITU-R BT.709 Luminance Grayscale & Document Scan Binarization Work

Why Weighted Perceptual Luminance Beats Simple RGB Averaging

Converting a full-color 24-bit RGB photograph into an authentic 8-bit grayscale image requires more than averaging (R + G + B) / 3. Because human eyes contain far more green-sensitive cone receptors than blue-sensitive ones, a pure green pixel appears roughly three times brighter to human vision than an equally intense blue pixel. Broadcast and digital imaging standards (ITU-R BT.709) solve this by weighting the green channel at 71.52%, red at 21.26%, and blue at 7.22%. Beyond artistic portrait photography, converting smartphone document scans or handwritten signatures to high-contrast grayscale removes yellow paper shadows, cuts file sizes, and improves OCR text accuracy for government exam portals.

ITU-R BT.709 Luminance Grayscale Formula + Worked Example

Each pixel's linear grayscale brightness Y (from 0 black to 255 white) is computed using standard HDTV perceptual weights:

Grayscale Luminance (Y) = 0.2126 × R + 0.7152 × G + 0.0722 × B

Worked Example: Consider a vibrant emerald green pixel with RGB values R = 16, G = 185, B = 129.

Weighted Luminance Y = (0.2126 × 16) + (0.7152 × 185) + (0.0722 × 129) = 3.40 + 132.31 + 9.31 = 145.02.

Setting R = G = B = 145 preserves the exact natural brightness contrast against surrounding darker or lighter colors.
