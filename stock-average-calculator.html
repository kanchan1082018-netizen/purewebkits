How Stock Averaging, Weighted Cost Basis & Target Averaging Work

What Is Stock Averaging (Dollar-Cost & Rupee-Cost Averaging)?

When you buy shares of the same company or ETF across different dates and price levels, your true cost basis is not a simple midpoint of the share prices—it is a quantity-weighted average price. Investors use averaging down (buying additional shares after a price dip) to lower their breakeven threshold, or pyramiding / averaging up (adding to winning stocks during an uptrend) while keeping strict control over total capital deployed. Knowing your exact weighted average price helps you set accurate stop-losses, calculate capital gains taxes, and avoid over-allocating capital to a single stock position.

Weighted Average & Target Quantity Formulas + Worked Example

Brokers and portfolio trackers compute your weighted average buy price and target averaging quantity using two exact algebraic equations:

Average Price = Σ(Sharesi × Pricei) / ΣSharesi  |  Extra Shares = Total Shares × (Avg − Target) / (Target − CMP)

Worked Example: Suppose you first buy 100 shares at ₹500 (₹50,000) and later buy 50 shares at ₹400 (₹20,000) during a market pullback.

Total Invested Capital = ₹50,000 + ₹20,000 = ₹70,000 across 100 + 50 = 150 shares.

Weighted Average Buy Price = ₹70,000 / 150 = ₹466.67 per share. If the stock trades at CMP = ₹420 and you want to lower your average to ₹450, you need 150 × (466.67 − 450) / (450 − 420) = 84 additional shares.
