Complete User Guide to UK PAYE Income Tax, National Insurance, Personal Allowance & Take-Home Pay

How Pay As You Earn (PAYE) Works on UK Employment Income

In the United Kingdom, most employees have Income Tax and Employee National Insurance deducted automatically from their pay before they receive it under the Pay As You Earn (PAYE) system. Your take-home pay depends on your gross salary, your tax-free Personal Allowance, applicable income tax rate bands (which differ between England/Wales/Northern Ireland and Scotland), employee National Insurance thresholds and rates, and any workplace pension contributions. Because salary and deduction numbers are personal, a browser-based UK PAYE Tax Calculator computes all estimates 100% locally in your browser using JavaScript—with zero server uploads.

How to Estimate UK Take-Home Pay in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Enter Gross Salary & Tax Region: Type your gross annual salary in pounds (£) and choose whether to model standard England/Wales/NI bands or Scottish bands, or click Load Sample (£45,000).
  2. Set Pension & Personal Allowance: Adjust your pension contribution percentage (%) and Personal Allowance (defaulting to £12,570, with automatic taper above £100,000 in this model).
  3. Review Monthly & Annual Figures: Inspect monthly net pay, annual Income Tax, National Insurance, and pension totals, then click Copy PAYE Summary or Download .txt.

PAYE Taxable Income & Net Pay Formula

Using gross salary G, pension deduction P_ded, tapered Personal Allowance PA, Income Tax IT, and Employee National Insurance NI:

Taxable Income = max(0, G − P_ded − PA)
Annual Net Pay = G − P_ded − IT − NI  |  Monthly Net Pay = Annual Net Pay / 12

Helpful Notes When Reviewing UK Payslip Estimates

  • Personal Allowance Taper Above £100,000: In standard UK rules, the Personal Allowance reduces by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000 until reaching £0 at £125,140.
  • Pension Relief & Student Loans: Real payslips can vary depending on relief-at-source vs. net-pay pension arrangements, student loan repayment plans, taxable benefits, or mid-year tax code changes.
  • Verify Tax Years & HMRC Codes: Tax thresholds and rates can be updated in UK budgets; verify exact figures against official HMRC guidance or your payslip.

Worked Example: For a gross salary of £45,000 (England/Wales/NI bands) with a 5% pension contribution (£2,250.00) and a £12,570 Personal Allowance, estimated annual Income Tax is £5,436.00 and Employee National Insurance is £2,226.00, leaving roughly £35,088.00/yr (£2,924.00/mo) net.
