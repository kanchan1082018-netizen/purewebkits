Complete User Guide to Browser Fingerprinting, Canvas/WebGL Signals & Privacy Hardening

How Browser Fingerprinting Combines Everyday Web APIs Into an Identifier

Even when you block third-party cookies or browse in an incognito window, a web browser exposes dozens of technical attributes through standard JavaScript APIs—including your User-Agent / Client Hints, screen resolution and device pixel ratio, time zone and locale preferences, CPU core count (navigator.hardwareConcurrency), touch support, Do Not Track / Global Privacy Control flags, and subtle rendering differences in HTML5 Canvas 2D and WebGL graphics drivers. When combined into a single composite signature, these signals can carry tens of bits of identifying entropy—meaning 2H possible combinations. Because all of these checks run directly inside your browser sandbox, you can inspect what scripts can see about your environment without ever transmitting a single byte to an external server. A browser-based Browser Fingerprint Inspector runs 100% locally in your browser—with zero server uploads.

How to Inspect & Export Your Browser Attributes in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Review Your Composite Hash & Entropy Score: When the page loads, the inspector reads your browser and hardware attributes locally and displays a composite hash alongside estimated signal entropy in bits.
  2. Inspect Individual Signal Cards & Canvas Probe: Scan each of the 12 attribute cards (User Agent, Platform/Hints, Screen & DPR, Timezone & Locale, Hardware Concurrency, Memory, Touch & Pointer, Color Scheme, Cookies & DNT/GPC, Canvas 2D Hash, WebGL Renderer, and Audio/Storage APIs).
  3. Re-Scan After Changing Window Size or Export Report: Resize your window or toggle dark/light theme and click Re-Scan Signals to see how viewport or preference signals change, then export via Copy Fingerprint JSON or Download .txt.

Signal Entropy & Combinatorial Space Formula

Summing estimated entropy bits bk across independent signal categories gives total approximate entropy H:

Total Estimated Entropy (H) = Σ bk (bits)  |  Distinct Combinations = 2H
Example: 32 bits → ~4.29 billion combinations • 40 bits → ~1.10 trillion combinations

Helpful Tips for Minimizing Unnecessary Fingerprinting Exposure

  • Use Privacy-Hardened Browsers When Needed: Browsers such as Firefox (with ResistFingerprinting / Enhanced Tracking Protection), Brave (Shields), or Tor Browser standardize or jitter high-entropy signals like Canvas, WebGL, and screen metrics.
  • Avoid Exotic Window Sizes or Niche Extensions When Testing Consistency: Standard viewport sizes and clean profiles blend into larger user cohorts.
  • Remember Legitimate Uses vs. Tracking: Security tools and fraud-prevention systems sometimes inspect device signals to detect automated bots or session hijacking, while ad trackers use them to link visits across sites.

Worked Example: Combining screen resolution + DPR (~4.5 bits), timezone + locale (~5.5 bits), WebGL GPU string (~6.0 bits), Canvas 2D hash (~6.0 bits), and user-agent/hardware hints (~8.0 bits) yields roughly 30–36 bits of combined entropy—illustrating why multiple small attributes add up quickly.
