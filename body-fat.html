Complete User Guide to Body Fat Percentage, U.S. Navy Circumference Method, and Lean Mass

Why Body Fat Percentage Adds Context Beyond Body Weight Alone

Standard body weight and height-based ratios alone cannot distinguish between lean tissue (such as skeletal muscle, organs, and bone) and adipose tissue. Two people with the exact same height and weight can have noticeably different body compositions depending on training background, frame, and genetics. A Body Fat Calculator using the U.S. Navy circumference method estimates body fat percentage from tape measurements of the neck, waist, and (for females) hip alongside height, translating that percentage into estimated fat mass and lean body mass. Because all math runs 100% client-side in your browser using JavaScript, your personal body measurements never leave your device.

How to Estimate Body Fat Percentage in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Select Units & Sex Formula: Choose Metric (kg / cm) or Imperial (lb / in) and select Male or Female so the proper U.S. Navy equation is applied.
  2. Enter Accurate Circumference Measurements: Measure the neck just below the larynx, the waist horizontally at the navel (or narrowest point as specified), and the hip at the widest point for female estimates, keeping the tape level and snug without compressing skin.
  3. Inspect Body Composition & Export: Review your estimated body fat percentage, fat mass, lean body mass, and waist-to-height ratio, then copy or download a plain-text summary if desired.

U.S. Navy Body Fat Equations (Metric in cm)

With height H, neck N, waist W, and hip P measured in centimeters (log10 denotes base-10 logarithm):

Male %BF = 86.010 × log10(W − N) − 70.041 × log10(H) + 36.76
Female %BF = 163.205 × log10(W + P − N) − 97.684 × log10(H) − 78.387

Measurement Accuracy & Health Context Tips

  • Take Repeated Measurements Consistently: Measuring at the same time of day—such as in the morning before large meals—reduces day-to-day bloat and tape-placement variation.
  • Remember Circumference Estimates Are Approximations: Tape formulas provide practical trend estimates rather than clinical DEXA or air-displacement plethysmography scans; consult a healthcare professional for medical evaluation.
  • Track Trends Over Weeks Rather Than Days: Focus on gradual multi-week direction rather than single-day fluctuations.

Worked Example: For a male with 75 kg weight, 175 cm height, 38 cm neck, and 84 cm waist (W − N = 46 cm), the U.S. Navy formula estimates roughly 18.4% body fat—corresponding to about 13.8 kg fat mass and 61.2 kg lean body mass.
