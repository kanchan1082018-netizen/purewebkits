Complete User Guide to Hijri (Islamic) and Gregorian Calendar Date Conversions

How the Lunar Hijri Calendar Differs From the Solar Gregorian Calendar

The Islamic (Hijri) calendar—abbreviated AH (Anno Hegirae, counting from the Hijra in 622 CE)—is a purely lunar calendar consisting of 12 lunar months of 29 or 30 days each. Because a lunar year of roughly 354 or 355 days is about 10 to 11 days shorter than a solar Gregorian year of 365.2425 days, Hijri months shift earlier across the Gregorian seasons by roughly 11 days every year. In everyday practice, moon sighting and regional announcements can shift a month start by a day compared with arithmetic or astronomical models—which is why an optional −1 / 0 / +1 day adjustment is helpful. Helpful Tips for Hijri Date Planning

  • Verify Local Crescent Sighting for Religious Observances: Dates for Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, and Muharram depend on local moon sighting or official national authorities and may vary by a day.
  • Check Day Bounds (1–30): In the tabular Hijri model, odd months have 30 days and even months have 29 days (except the 12th month, Dhu al-Hijjah, which has 30 days in tabular leap years).
  • Use "Today" for Quick Reference: Click Today anytime to see today's date in both calendars.
