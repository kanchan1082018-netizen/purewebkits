How US Paycheck Withholding, Federal Brackets & FICA Taxes Work

Understanding Gross Salary vs. Net Take-Home Paycheck

When negotiating a US job offer or switching from hourly wages to an annual salary, your gross pay is only the starting number. Before your paycheck hits direct deposit, employers withhold mandatory payroll taxes and voluntary pre-tax benefits. First, pre-tax deductions—such as Traditional 401(k) retirement contributions and employer-sponsored health insurance or HSA premiums—are subtracted from gross pay, lowering your federal taxable income. Next, progressive Federal Income Tax, flat-rate FICA payroll taxes (6.2% Social Security up to the annual wage cap plus 1.45% Medicare), and applicable State Income Tax are withheld based on your W-4 filing status and work state.

Net Paycheck Mathematical Formula & Worked Example

Payroll systems compute per-paycheck net take-home pay by dividing annual tax obligations across the number of annual pay periods (N):

Net Paycheck = Gross Paycheck − Pre-Tax Deductions − (Federal Tax + FICA + State Tax)

Worked Example: Suppose a Single employee in Texas (0% state income tax) earns an annual salary of $80,000 paid Bi-Weekly (26 paychecks/year), contributing 5% to a 401(k) with $50/paycheck in medical insurance.

Bi-Weekly Gross Pay = $80,000 / 26 = $3,076.92. Pre-Tax Deductions = $153.85 (401k) + $50.00 (Medical) = $203.85.

After subtracting Federal withholding (~$346) and FICA payroll taxes (~$231.56), the employee receives approximately $2,295.51 net take-home pay every two weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

FICA (Federal Insurance Contributions Act) consists of two mandatory payroll taxes: 6.2% for Social Security (applied on wages up to the annual IRS wage base limit of $176,100) and 1.45% for Medicare (applied on all wages, plus an extra 0.9% above $200,000 for single filers). Your employer also pays a matching 7.65%.
Traditional 401(k) and Section 125 health insurance/HSA contributions are deducted before Federal and State income taxes are calculated. For example, if you are in the 22% federal bracket, contributing $200 per paycheck to a Traditional 401(k) reduces your federal tax withholding by $44 on that same paycheck.
Bi-Weekly pays every two weeks (52 weeks ÷ 2 = 26 paychecks per year, meaning two months each year have 3 paydays). Semi-Monthly pays twice per month—usually on the 1st and 15th—resulting in 24 slightly larger paychecks per year.
