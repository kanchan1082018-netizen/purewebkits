Complete User Guide to Readability Scores, Flesch-Kincaid Grade Levels, and Clear Writing

Why Measuring Readability Improves Engagement and Content Clarity

Online readers scan pages quickly, and dense walls of text packed with long, multi-clause sentences and heavy jargon cause visitors to bounce before reaching your key point. A Readability Score Checker evaluates objective linguistic signals—average sentence length, syllable density per word, and complex multi-syllable words—to estimate how easy your writing is to understand. Whether you are polishing a blog post, landing page, user guide, newsletter, or school essay, checking your Flesch Reading Ease and grade-level indices helps you spot overly tangled sentences and tighten your phrasing. Because this analyzer runs 100% client-side in your browser using JavaScript, your unpublished drafts remain completely private on your device.

How to Check Your Text's Readability in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Paste or Type Your Draft: Enter at least two or three complete sentences into the Article / Draft Text Input box, or click Load Sample Article to inspect an example breakdown.
  2. Review Readability Indices & Averages: Check your Flesch Reading Ease score (0–100 scale), Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level, Gunning Fog Index, Automated Readability Index (ARI), and average words per sentence.
  3. Simplify Long Sentences & Export Report: Split sentences exceeding 20–25 words, swap unnecessary jargon for everyday words, and click Copy Readability Report or Download .txt when done.

Flesch Reading Ease & Flesch-Kincaid Grade Formulas

Both classic Flesch readability equations combine words per sentence (ASL) and syllables per word (ASW):

Flesch Reading Ease = 206.835 − (1.015 × Words/Sentences) − (84.6 × Syllables/Words)
Flesch-Kincaid Grade = (0.39 × Words/Sentences) + (11.8 × Syllables/Words) − 15.59

Actionable Tips to Improve Readability Scores

  • Target 60–70+ Flesch Reading Ease for Web Content: Scores in this range correspond roughly to 7th–9th grade reading ease, making articles approachable for broad audiences without oversimplifying ideas.
  • Keep Average Sentence Length Under 18–20 Words: Breaking compound sentences into two crisp sentences immediately improves both Flesch and Gunning Fog scores.
  • Use Punctuation Properly: End sentences with periods, question marks, or exclamation points so sentence-counting rules measure average sentence length accurately.

Worked Example: A 60-word passage across 4 sentences (15.0 words/sentence) with 84 syllables (1.40 syllables/word) yields a Flesch Reading Ease score of 206.835 − 15.225 − 118.44 = 73.2 and a Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level of roughly 6.8.
