Complete User Guide to Podcast Episode Planning, Run-of-Show Timestamps & Show Notes

How Segment Pacing and Cumulative Timestamps Keep Episodes Tight and Engaging

Great podcast episodes rarely happen by accident—they rely on a clear run-of-show that balances a concise cold open or intro, focused topic segments, natural transitions, and a memorable wrap-up while respecting listeners' time. Mapping each segment's planned duration in minutes produces automatic cumulative timestamps (MM:SS) for both recording pacing and post-production chapter markers or YouTube/podcast show notes. Comparing your total planned runtime against a target episode length also reveals immediately whether your outline is running tight or over-scoped before you hit record. A browser-based Podcast Episode Planner runs 100% locally in your browser—with zero server uploads.

How to Plan a Podcast Episode in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Set Episode Metadata & Target Runtime: Enter your show name, episode title, guest/co-host name, and target episode length in minutes.
  2. Add & Reorder Run-of-Show Segments: Add segments with durations and talking points—use the Up / Down buttons to reorder segments and watch cumulative start times update automatically.
  3. Copy or Download Timestamped Show Notes: Review the live show-notes preview and click Copy Show Notes or Download .txt for your recording doc or episode description.

Cumulative Timestamp & Speaking Pace Formula

At a natural conversational speaking rate of ~145 words per minute and cumulative segment durations d1, d2, ...:

Start Time of Segment k = Σi=1..k−1 di  |  Approx. Spoken Words ≈ Total_Minutes × 145

Helpful Tips for Smooth Recording & Editing

  • Leave Breathing Room for Banter & Follow-Ups: Conversational interviews naturally expand around strong stories—leaving a few minutes of buffer helps hit your target length cleanly.
  • Capture Key Takeaways as You Plan: Writing a crisp one-line summary per segment makes editing chapters and writing episode descriptions much faster later.
  • Keep Segments Focused on One Idea at a Time: Breaking long topics into distinct timed beats keeps listener attention high across longer episodes.

Worked Example: An episode with a 2.0m intro starting at 00:00, a 12.0m main interview starting at 02:00, and an 8.0m Q&A starting at 14:00 totals 22.0 minutes (22:00) and corresponds to roughly 3,190 spoken words at ~145 wpm.
