Complete User Guide to Random Number Generation, Ranges, Uniqueness, and Sorting

Why Random Number Generators Are Useful for Sampling, Games, and Testing

Generating unbiased random numbers is essential whenever you need fair chance without manual bias—such as picking raffle or giveaway winners, running statistical simulations and Monte Carlo trials, generating test cases for software, shuffling lists, or rolling dice and numeric draws. Using your own intuition to "pick a random number" often clusters around familiar numbers rather than distributing evenly across a range. A browser-based Random Number Generator uses cryptographic randomness (crypto.getRandomValues() with rejection sampling when available) to produce uniform integers or two-decimal numbers—100% locally in your browser with zero server uploads.

How to Generate Random Numbers in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Set Minimum & Maximum Bounds: Enter your lower bound in Minimum Value and upper bound in Maximum Value (make sure Min ≤ Max).
  2. Choose Count, Type & Options: Select how many numbers to generate (1 to 500), pick Integer (Whole Number) or Decimal (2 Places), and toggle Unique numbers only or Sort results ascending.
  3. Generate, Copy or Export: Click Generate Again for fresh draws, then click Copy Numbers or Download .txt.

Uniform Integer & Decimal Sampling Formulas

For inclusive integer bounds [min, max] or continuous decimal bounds [min, max] with uniform fraction u ∈ [0, 1):

Integer in [min, max]: min + floor(u × (max − min + 1))
Decimal in [min, max]: round((min + u × (max − min)) × 100) / 100

Helpful Tips When Generating Random Numbers

  • Check Pool Size When Unique Is Enabled: For integer ranges, there are at most max − min + 1 distinct integers available, so the requested count cannot exceed that pool size.
  • Use Sorting for Ordered Draws: Enabling Sort results ascending arranges generated numbers from smallest to largest for easier reading.
  • Note Use Case Suitability: Client-side browser generation is great for everyday draws, testing, and simulations; specialized cryptographic systems follow dedicated security engineering standards.

Worked Example: Generating 5 unique integers between 1 and 100 with ascending sort produces five distinct whole numbers in [1, 100] ordered from lowest to highest.
