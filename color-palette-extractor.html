How K-Means Color Quantization & Dominant Brand Palette Extraction Work


      
Turning Inspiration Photos & Brand Logos into Cohesive UI Color Systems

      

        Every great brand identity, website theme, or illustration relies on a harmonious set of dominant, secondary, and accent colors. However, a standard 12-megapixel photograph contains hundreds of thousands of subtle pixel variations caused by lighting gradients and camera sensor noise. Our browser-based Color Palette Generator samples your uploaded image onto an offscreen HTML5 Canvas and runs an iterative K-Means clustering color quantization algorithm—grouping visually similar RGB pixels around their nearest cluster centroids and ranking them by pixel coverage percentage. Within milliseconds, you receive clean 6-digit HEX codes, RGB triplets, a ready-to-paste CSS :root variable block, and a downloadable PNG swatch card.

3D Euclidean RGB Color Distance Formula + Worked Example

      

        To assign each sampled pixel (R1, G1, B1) to its closest palette centroid (R2, G2, B2), the clustering engine minimizes 3D Euclidean color distance:
      

      

        Color Distance (ΔERGB) = √[ (R2 − R1)² + (G2 − G1)² + (B2 − B1)² ]
      


      

        
Worked Example: Suppose an image pixel has RGB(35, 102, 232) and two candidate palette centroids are Brand Blue RGB(37, 99, 235) and Slate Dark RGB(15, 23, 42).

        
Distance to Brand Blue = √[ (37−35)² + (99−102)² + (235−232)² ] = √(4 + 9 + 9) = 4.69.

        
Distance to Slate Dark = √[ (15−35)² + (23−102)² + (42−232)² ] = 206.74 → The pixel joins the Brand Blue (#2563EB) cluster.
