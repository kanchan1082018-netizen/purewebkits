Complete User Guide to Data Storage Units, SI Decimal (KB/MB/GB) vs. IEC Binary (KiB/MiB/GiB)

Why Decimal and Binary Storage Units Differ and Why It Matters

Digital storage capacity is measured in bits and bytes, scaled up either by powers of ten (SI decimal prefixes: KB, MB, GB, TB, PB) or by powers of two (IEC binary prefixes: KiB, MiB, GiB, TiB, where 1 KiB = 1,024 bytes). Storage manufacturers and networking specs often use decimal powers of 1,000, while operating systems and memory addresses frequently report binary powers of 1,024—creating a noticeable gap (such as 1 GB = 1,000,000,000 B vs. 1 GiB = 1,073,741,824 B). Helpful Tips When Working With File Sizes & Storage

  • Watch Bits vs. Bytes: Network speeds are commonly quoted in bits (Mbps/Gbps), while file sizes and storage capacities are quoted in bytes (8 bits = 1 byte).
  • Compare GB vs. GiB When Checking Disk Space: A 1 TB drive (10¹² bytes) equals roughly 0.9095 TiB in binary gibibytes/tebibytes.
  • Use All-Units View for Quick Cross-Checks: Scan the live list to see how a value looks in bytes, SI units, and IEC binary units simultaneously.

Worked Example: Converting 1 SI Gigabyte (1 GB = 10⁹ bytes) into SI Megabytes gives 1,000 MB, whereas in binary IEC units it equals approximately 953.674 MiB or 0.931323 GiB.
