Complete User Guide to Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), TDEE, and Mifflin-St Jeor Estimates

What Is Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and How Does It Differ From TDEE?

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is an estimate of the daily energy your body expends at complete rest in a thermoneutral environment to sustain vital involuntary functions such as breathing, circulation, cell maintenance, and brain activity. Because everyday life involves standing, walking, working, and exercising on top of resting metabolism, multiplying BMR by an activity factor yields an estimate of Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)—often referred to as maintenance calories. Understanding both numbers helps people build realistic nutrition and fitness plans, though individual metabolism varies with muscle mass, genetics, age, sleep, and medical factors. Mifflin-St Jeor BMR & TDEE Equations

In metric units (weight W in kg, height H in cm, age A in years), the widely used Mifflin-St Jeor equations are:

BMR (Male) = (10 × W) + (6.25 × H) − (5 × A) + 5
BMR (Female) = (10 × W) + (6.25 × H) − (5 × A) − 161
TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor

Practical Context & Safe Nutrition Reminders

  • Treat Equations as Starting Estimates: Formula-based BMR and TDEE are statistical estimates, not lab measurements from indirect calorimetry—adjust gradually based on real-world progress over several weeks.
  • Avoid Extreme Calorie Cuts: Very low intake far below BMR can cause fatigue, nutrient shortfalls, and poor adherence; consult a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian for personalized medical or clinical guidance.
  • Re-Evaluate as Body Weight Changes: Recalculate periodically as your weight or activity routine changes over time.

Worked Example: For a 28-year-old male weighing 70 kg and standing 175 cm tall, BMR = (10 × 70) + (6.25 × 175) − (5 × 28) + 5 = 1,673.75 kcal/day. At a Lightly Active multiplier of 1.375, estimated TDEE is roughly 2,301 kcal/day.
