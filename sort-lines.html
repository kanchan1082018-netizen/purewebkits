Complete User Guide to Sorting Text Lines, Natural Number Order, and List Cleanup

Why Sorting Text Lines Quickly Matters in Everyday Workflows

Whether you are organizing a list of URLs, alphabetizing glossary entries, ordering numbered task items, comparing two sets of lines, or cleaning up exported records, sorting text lines by hand is tedious and error-prone. Standard ASCII or lexicographical sorting often places Item 10 ahead of Item 2 because "1" comes before "2" character by character, which can confuse human readers. A browser-based Sort Text Lines utility lets you sort lines alphabetically (A → Z or Z → A), apply natural numeric ordering (1 → 10), order lines by character length, or shuffle them randomly—all locally inside your browser without sending your lists to any remote server.

How to Sort Text Lines in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Paste Your Lines: Enter or paste your multi-line list into the Input Lines box, or click Load Sample Lines to see how each mode behaves.
  2. Select a Sort Mode & Cleanup Options: Choose alphabetical, natural numeric, line-length, or random sorting, and toggle Skip blank lines or Remove duplicates as needed.
  3. Copy or Download Your Sorted List: Click Copy Sorted Lines to copy the result to your clipboard or download it as a .txt file.

Ordering & Deduplication Pipeline Formula

Each run normalizes line endings, optionally filters empty or duplicate lines, and applies a deterministic (or Fisher–Yates shuffled) comparator across the line array:

Lines = split(text.replace(/\r\n?/g, "\n"), "\n") → filter(blank?, duplicate?) → sort(comparator)

Tips for Choosing the Right Sort Order

  • Use Natural / Numeric for Numbered Items: When lines contain numbers like Step 2 and Step 10, natural sorting places Step 2 before Step 10.
  • Remove Duplicates Before or After Sorting: Enabling Remove duplicates keeps unique entries so repeated lines do not clutter your final list.
  • Combine With Line Break or Whitespace Tools: If your list has extra spaces or stray line breaks, clean those up with companion PureWebKits text utilities.

Worked Example: Given lines item 10, item 2, and item 1, choosing Natural / Numeric (1 → 10) orders them as item 1, item 2, and item 10, whereas Alphabetical (A → Z) orders item 10 before item 2.
