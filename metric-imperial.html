Complete User Guide to Metric and Imperial Unit Conversions (Length, Mass, Volume & Temperature)

How the Metric (SI) and Imperial Systems Differ

The Metric system (based on the International System of Units, or SI) scales units in clean decimal factors of 10, 100, and 1,000—such as millimeters, centimeters, meters, and kilometers for length; grams and kilograms for mass; and milliliters and liters for volume. The Imperial / US customary systems use historical and practical step ratios—such as 12 inches per foot, 3 feet per yard, 1,760 yards (5,280 feet) per mile, 16 ounces per pound, and 14 pounds per stone—while temperature scales compare Celsius (°C), Fahrenheit (°F), and Kelvin (K) on offset linear scales. Exact International Conversion Anchors & Temperature Equations

Standard international definitions anchor linear conversions, while temperature uses affine formulas:

1 in = 2.54 cm = 0.0254 m  |  1 lb = 0.45359237 kg  |  1 mi = 1.609344 km
°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32  |  °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9  |  K = °C + 273.15

Helpful Tips for Accurate Unit Conversions

  • Distinguish US Gallons vs. UK Imperial Gallons: 1 US gallon is 3.785411784 L, whereas 1 UK imperial gallon is 4.54609 L.
  • Keep Full Precision During Multi-Step Math: Avoid rounding intermediate figures until the final result so rounding errors do not accumulate.
  • Allow Negative Values Only Where Physically Meaningful: Temperatures can be negative (such as −10 °C), whereas positive lengths, masses, and volumes are standard.

Worked Example: Converting 10 km to miles gives 10 / 1.609344 ≈ 6.2137 mi (which equals 10,000 m or 32,808.40 ft).
