How Loan EMI Calculation, Reducing Balance Interest & Amortization Work

Understanding Reducing-Balance EMI vs. Flat Interest Rates

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is the fixed monthly payment you make to a bank or lender until your loan is paid off in full. Nearly all regulated home loans, auto loans, and personal loans use the reducing-balance method: every monthly EMI consists of two parts—an interest portion calculated on your remaining unpaid principal, and a principal repayment portion that lowers your outstanding balance. During the early years of a long-term mortgage, interest accounts for the majority of each EMI; as your principal balance shrinks, a progressively larger share of the same fixed EMI goes toward clearing principal.

Standard Reducing-Balance EMI Mathematical Formula & Worked Example

Banks and financial institutions calculate exact monthly EMI using the compound reducing-balance annuity formula:

EMI = [ P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ] / [ (1 + r)ⁿ − 1 ]

Where P = Loan Principal Amount, r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate / 12 / 100), and n = Total Number of Monthly Installments (Years × 12).

Worked Example: Suppose you borrow a ₹10,00,000 (10 Lakh) car or business loan at 9.0% annual interest for a tenure of 5 years (60 months).

Monthly interest rate r = 9.0 / 12 / 100 = 0.0075, and total installments n = 60.

Applying the formula: EMI = [10,00,000 × 0.0075 × (1.0075)⁶⁰] / [(1.0075)⁶⁰ − 1] = ₹20,758 per month. Over 60 months, total repayment equals ₹12,45,501 (₹10,00,000 principal + ₹2,45,501 total interest).

Frequently Asked Questions

Extending your loan tenure (for example, from 15 years to 25 years) lowers your monthly EMI payment, making monthly cash flow easier. However, because interest accrues on the unpaid principal for a longer duration, your total interest paid over the life of the loan increases substantially.
Most banks and financial planners recommend keeping your combined monthly EMIs within 35% to 40% of your net monthly take-home income. Staying below 40% ensures comfortable living expenses, emergency savings, and smooth loan approval.
Making even one extra EMI prepayment per year directly toward your principal reduces your outstanding balance early, shaving several years off a 20-year home loan and saving lakhs in cumulative interest.
