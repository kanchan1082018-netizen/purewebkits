Complete User Guide to Satoshis (sats), Bitcoin Denominations (BTC, mBTC, Bits) & Fiat Conversion

What Is a Satoshi and Why Bitcoin Uses 8 Decimal Places

A Satoshi (commonly abbreviated sat or sats) is the smallest on-chain divisible unit of Bitcoin (BTC), named after Bitcoin's pseudonymous creator, Satoshi Nakamoto. By protocol design, exactly 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Satoshis equal 1.00000000 BTC, meaning 1 satoshi = 0.00000001 BTC (10−8 BTC). Because whole-Bitcoin prices are large numbers, wallets, Lightning Network apps, fee estimators (sat/vB), and micro-payment tools frequently denominate amounts in sats, millibitcoin (mBTC), or bits (μBTC) alongside a local fiat equivalent. A browser-based Satoshi Calculator converts across all five units 100% locally in your browser using JavaScript—with zero server uploads.

Exact Bitcoin & Satoshi Conversion Equations

Every Bitcoin sub-unit is an exact power-of-ten multiple of a single Satoshi:

1 BTC = 1,000 mBTC = 1,000,000 bits (μBTC) = 100,000,000 sats
Fiat Value = (Satoshis / 100,000,000) × Price_1_BTC  |  Sats per 1 Fiat = 100,000,000 / Price_1_BTC

Helpful Tips When Working With Satoshis & Transaction Fees

  • Understand "Satoshis Parity": When 1 BTC reaches $100,000,000, 1 sat equals $1.00; at $100,000 per BTC, 1,000 sats equals $1.00.
  • Check On-Chain Fee Rates in sat/vB: Bitcoin network fees are quoted in satoshis per virtual byte (sat/vB)—multiplying sat/vB by transaction virtual size gives total fee in sats, which you can convert to fiat here.
  • Enter Your Exchange's Live BTC Quote: Because crypto prices move continuously, enter the exact BTC quote you want to model in 1 BTC Price (in Fiat).

Worked Example: At a reference price of $90,000.00 per BTC, 100,000 sats equals 0.00100000 BTC (1.00000 mBTC or 1,000 bits), worth $90.00, while $1.00 buys 1,111.11 sats.
