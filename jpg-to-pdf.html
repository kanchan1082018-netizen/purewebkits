How Browser-Based JPG & PNG to PDF Conversion Works

Why Convert Multiple Photos into a Single Standardized PDF?

Modern smartphones capture receipts, signed contracts, academic certificates, and government ID cards as separate .jpg or .png image files. However, university admission portals, tax e-filing systems, court clerks, and HR onboarding platforms almost always require a single consolidated .pdf file formatted to standard paper dimensions. Converting images to PDF locks page sequence, prevents accidental image cropping across different devices, and ensures clean print scaling on standard A4 or US Letter paper. Because PureWebKits builds the PDF locally in your browser's memory using pdf-lib, sensitive documents like passports, bank statements, and medical bills never touch an external cloud server.

Image-to-PDF Aspect Ratio Scaling Formula & Worked Example

When placing a high-resolution photo onto a standardized PDF page without stretching or distortion, the converter calculates a uniform proportional scale factor using PDF points (where 1 inch = 72 points):

Scale Factor = min( (Page Width − 2 × Margin) / Image Width , (Page Height − 2 × Margin) / Image Height )

Worked Example: Suppose you photograph 3 pages of a signed rental agreement on your phone, each measuring 2480 × 3508 pixels (portrait), and want them centered on standard A4 paper (595.28 × 841.89 pt) with a 20 pt margin on all sides.

Available printable box width = 595.28 − 40 = 555.28 pt; available height = 841.89 − 40 = 801.89 pt.

Scale factor = min(555.28 / 2480, 801.89 / 3508) = min(0.2239, 0.2285) = 0.2239. Every photo scales cleanly to 555.28 × 785.44 pt, centered symmetrically on each A4 page with zero distortion.
