Complete User Guide to Fraction Arithmetic, Common Denominators, and Simplification

Why Fraction Calculators Make Math, Recipes, and Measurements Faster

Working with fractions comes up in everyday tasks—scaling cooking recipes, measuring dimensions on a ruler or tape measure, checking proportions, solving homework problems, and verifying engineering or woodworking tolerances. Adding and subtracting fractions requires finding a common denominator, while dividing fractions involves multiplying by the reciprocal, and every result should be reduced to lowest terms using the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD). A browser-based Fraction Calculator performs addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division automatically, displaying the simplified fraction, mixed number (when applicable), decimal equivalent, and percentage—100% locally in your browser with zero server uploads.

Fraction Arithmetic & Reduction Formulas

Given two fractions a/b and c/d (with non-zero denominators):

Add / Sub: (a/b) ± (c/d) = (ad ± bc) / (bd) → divide by gcd(num, den)
Multiply: (a/b) × (c/d) = (ac) / (bd)  |  Divide: (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (ad) / (bc)

Practical Tips When Working With Fractions

  • Denominators Cannot Be Zero: Division by zero is undefined in arithmetic, so make sure every denominator is a non-zero integer.
  • Check Mixed Numbers for Improper Fractions: When the magnitude of the numerator exceeds the denominator (such as 7/4), the mixed number view shows 1 3/4 for intuitive reading.
  • Use Decimal & Percentage Views for Comparisons: Converting fractions like 3/8 (0.375 or 37.5%) makes comparing quantities effortless.

Worked Example: Adding 1/2 and 1/3 over common denominator 6 gives 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6, which is already in simplest form and equals approximately 0.833333 (83.33%).
