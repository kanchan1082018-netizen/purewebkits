Complete User Guide to Greatest Common Divisor (GCD / HCF) and Least Common Multiple (LCM)

Why GCD and LCM Are Essential in Math, Fractions, and Scheduling

Two foundational concepts in integer arithmetic are the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)—also called the Highest Common Factor (HCF)—and the Least Common Multiple (LCM). The GCD is the largest positive integer that divides every number in a set with zero remainder, making it essential for reducing fractions to lowest terms and grouping items evenly. The LCM is the smallest positive integer divisible by every number in a set, making it indispensable when finding common denominators for fraction addition or aligning repeating cycles and schedules. Euclidean Algorithm & LCM Relation Formula

The Euclidean algorithm finds the GCD of two non-negative integers by repeatedly taking remainders until reaching zero, while LCM relates to GCD via their product:

gcd(a, b) = gcd(b, a mod b) until remainder = 0
lcm(a, b) = (|a| / gcd(a, b)) × |b|   (extended iteratively across multiple numbers)

Helpful Tips for Working With Common Factors & Multiples

  • Coprime Numbers Have a GCD of 1: When two integers share no common divisor greater than 1 (such as 8 and 15), their GCD is 1 and their LCM equals their product (120).
  • Use GCD to Simplify Fractions & Ratios: Dividing numerator and denominator by their GCD reduces ratios like 48:60 cleanly to 4:5.
  • Use LCM for Repeating Cycles & Denominators: If events repeat every 12 and 18 days, they next coincide on day lcm(12, 18) = 36.

Worked Example: For 12, 18, and 24, the largest number dividing all three is GCD = 6, and the smallest positive number divisible by all three is LCM = 72.
