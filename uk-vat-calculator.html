How UK VAT Calculation, HMRC Rates & Reverse VAT Work

Understanding Standard (20%), Reduced (5%) & Zero-Rated (0%) UK VAT

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax administered by HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) and applied across most goods and services sold in the United Kingdom. UK businesses whose VAT-taxable turnover exceeds the statutory £90,000 registration threshold over any rolling 12-month period must register for VAT, charge the applicable rate on customer invoices, and file quarterly Making Tax Digital (MTD) returns. The default 20% Standard Rate covers electronics, software, consulting, hospitality, and adult clothing, while the 5% Reduced Rate applies to domestic gas and electricity, children's car seats, and smoking cessation aids. Basic groceries, books, newspapers, and children's clothing qualify for the 0% Zero Rate.

How to Add or Remove UK VAT in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Choose Add or Remove Mode: Select + Add VAT when issuing a B2B invoice from a net price, or − Remove VAT when extracting the VAT element from a gross retail receipt.
  2. Enter Pound Amount & VAT Band: Type your figure in £ GBP and tap 20% Standard, 5% Reduced, 0% Zero Rate, or enter a custom percentage.
  3. Copy Exact Invoice Figures: Click Copy Net, Copy VAT, or Copy Gross to paste formatted figures directly into Xero, QuickBooks, Sage, or your PDF invoice.

Add VAT & Reverse VAT Mathematical Formulas + Worked Example

To avoid rounding errors when working backward from a VAT-inclusive receipt, HMRC accounting rules divide by 1 + VAT Rate (which equals 1.20 at 20% VAT, or multiplying by 1/6):

Add 20% VAT: Gross = Net × 1.20  |  Remove 20% VAT: Net = Gross / 1.20  (VAT = Gross − Net)

Worked Example (Reverse VAT Extraction): Suppose a UK contractor receives a VAT-inclusive supplier receipt for £600.00 Gross charged at the 20% Standard Rate.

Net Price (Excl. VAT) = £600.00 / 1.20 = £500.00.

Reclaimable Input VAT = £600.00 − £500.00 = £100.00 (exact 1/6th of the gross receipt).

Frequently Asked Questions

The UK VAT registration threshold is £90,000 of VAT-taxable turnover over any rolling 12-month period (with a voluntary deregistration threshold of £88,000). Businesses below £90,000 may still register voluntarily to reclaim input VAT on business equipment and expenses.
To remove 20% VAT from a VAT-inclusive price, divide the gross amount by 1.20 to find the net price, or divide the gross amount by 6 to find the exact VAT portion. For example, on a £240 gross bill, Net = £240 ÷ 1.20 = £200, and VAT = £240 ÷ 6 = £40.
Zero-rated goods (such as basic food and children's clothing) are still taxable supplies taxed at 0%, which means VAT-registered sellers can reclaim input VAT on related business purchases. Exempt supplies (such as insurance, financial services, and most education) sit outside VAT, meaning no VAT is charged and input VAT generally cannot be reclaimed.
