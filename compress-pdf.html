Understanding Browser-Based PDF Compression

How Client-Side PDF Compression Shrinks Large Scanned Files

Documents created by smartphone camera apps and flatbed office scanners often swell to several megabytes because each page is embedded as an uncompressed or lightly compressed 300 to 600 DPI raster image. While that high pixel density is meant for commercial printing, online application portals only need crisp screen legibility. Client-side PDF compression tackles this bloat directly inside your browser: Recommended and Extreme modes downsample oversized page images to practical 130 DPI or 96 DPI resolutions and apply efficient JPEG quantization. Depending on page content, this process cuts file weight by 60% to 85% while keeping signatures, forms, and stamps sharp. For born-digital PDFs that already rely on vector text, Low Compression strips hidden metadata, unused incremental saves, and unlinked objects while keeping text selectable.

PDF Compression Percentage Formula & Worked Example

To verify whether your document meets a portal's upload threshold and measure exact efficiency, calculate the percentage saved using the standard size-reduction formula:

Size Reduction (%) = ((Original Size − Compressed Size) / Original Size) × 100

Worked Example: Suppose you scan a 10-page property deed and academic verification packet on a multifunction printer, resulting in a 6.4 MB (6,553.6 KB) PDF. Your state e-filing portal strictly rejects attachments larger than 1 MB (1,024 KB).

By running the packet through Recommended Compression (130 DPI / 0.65 JPEG) in PureWebKits, each 300 DPI page image is cleanly downsampled and re-encoded, shrinking the finished PDF to 920 KB.

Applying the formula: ((6,553.6 − 920) / 6,553.6) × 100 = 85.96% (approximately 85.6% on a 6.4 MB decimal basis: ((6,400 − 920) / 6,400) × 100 = 85.6%). Your packet remains crisp enough for clerk review and passes the 1 MB e-filing upload limit effortlessly.
