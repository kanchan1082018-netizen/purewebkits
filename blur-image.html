How Block Mosaic Pixelation, Gaussian Blur & Irreversible Redaction Work

Protecting Bystander Faces, License Plates & Sensitive IDs Before Sharing Online

Sharing photos on social media, public forums, YouTube thumbnails, or customer support tickets frequently exposes private details—such as children's faces in school photos, vehicle license plates in street shots, or partial Aadhaar/PAN/bank account numbers on document screenshots. Under global privacy regulations including GDPR (Europe) and India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, anonymizing personally identifiable information (PII) before public publication protects both individuals and publishers. Unlike reversible sticker overlays that can sometimes be stripped from layered project files, our HTML5 Canvas engine overwrites the selected pixel matrix directly with averaged mosaic blocks, blurred convolution samples, or solid blackout bars—ensuring the original facial or text pixels cannot be reconstructed from the exported file.

Block Mosaic Downsampling Formula + Worked Example

Mosaic pixelation divides a selected region of width W and height H into square grid cells of block size B pixels, replacing all B² pixels in each cell with their mean RGB color:

Mosaic Grid Cells = ⌈ W / B ⌉ × ⌈ H / B ⌉  |  Cell Color = (1 / B²) Σ RGB(x, y)

Worked Example: Suppose you select a 180 × 216 px face region using a block size of B = 18 px.

The region collapses into just (180 / 18) × (216 / 18) = 10 × 12 = 120 uniform color squares.

Because 38,880 individual skin and eye pixels are averaged down to 120 flat blocks (a 324× information reduction), facial recognition algorithms and human viewers cannot recover the original identity.
