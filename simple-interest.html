Complete User Guide to Simple Interest Calculations, Time Units, and Total Maturity

How Simple Interest Differs From Compound Interest

Simple interest is calculated strictly on the original principal amount (P) across the entire borrowing or investment period—meaning earned or accrued interest does not compound onto itself in subsequent periods. Short-term loans, promissory notes, basic lending agreements, and quick educational comparisons frequently use simple interest because the interest accrual per year remains constant for a given principal and annual rate. A browser-based Simple Interest Calculator computes simple interest, total maturity/repayment amount, and monthly or annual equivalents across years, months, or days—100% locally in your browser with zero server uploads.

Simple Interest & Total Amount Formulas

With principal P, annual interest rate R%, and time in years T:

Simple Interest (SI) = (P × R × T) / 100
Total Amount (A) = P + SI  |  Annual Interest (1 Yr) = (P × R) / 100

Helpful Tips When Using Simple Interest

  • Convert Months or Days Into Years: Because the annual interest rate is per year (% p.a.), 6 months is 0.5 years (6/12) and 73 days is 0.2 years (73/365).
  • Check Day-Count Conventions for Short Loans: Some financial agreements use a 365-day year while others use a 360-day or 366-day basis; this calculator uses 365 days per year for day inputs.
  • Compare Against Compounding for Multi-Year Savings: For longer-term investments where interest compounds periodically, compound growth exceeds simple interest.

Worked Example: On a principal of $10,000.00 at an 8% annual interest rate for 3 years, simple interest is ($10,000 × 8 × 3) / 100 = $2,400.00 ($800.00/year or ~$66.67/month), giving a total amount of $12,400.00.
