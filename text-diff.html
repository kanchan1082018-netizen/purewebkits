Complete User Guide to Text Diff Comparison, Inline Redlines, and Draft Proofreading

Why Word-Level Text Comparison Is Ideal for Writers, Editors, and Teams

When reviewing edits to an article, contract clause, product description, email draft, or study summary, seeing only that an entire paragraph changed forces you to re-read every word to find what actually moved. A dedicated Text Diff utility highlights exact word-level insertions in green and struck-through deletions in red right inside the sentence context—while also offering a line-by-line view when comparing lists or structured paragraphs. Because everything executes 100% client-side inside your browser using JavaScript, confidential drafts, client documents, and unpublished copy never leave your computer.

How to Compare Two Text Versions in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Paste Original & Revised Text: Insert your baseline draft into Original Text (Draft A) and your updated version into Revised Text (Draft B), or click Load Sample Drafts.
  2. Choose Word or Line Granularity: Keep Word by Word (Inline Redlines) selected to inspect subtle phrasing edits inside sentences, or switch to Line by Line for bullet lists and multi-line notes.
  3. Review Similarity & Export Notes: Check the added/removed counters and similarity score, then click Copy Diff Summary or Download .txt to save a plain-text record of the changes.

Token Alignment & Similarity Percentage Formula

The comparison engine computes the Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) across word tokens (or lines) and estimates overlap using shared units relative to total units across both drafts:

Similarity (%) = [ (2 × Unchanged Tokens) / (Total Tokens in A + Total Tokens in B) ] × 100

Tips for Reviewing Text Edits Effectively

  • Use Word-by-Word Mode for Prose Edits: Inline redlines make single-word replacements, tense adjustments, and punctuation fixes stand out immediately inside long paragraphs.
  • Toggle Ignore Case When Checking Capitalization: Enable Ignore case if you want to focus strictly on wording changes rather than title-case adjustments.
  • Swap Drafts to Verify Direction: Use Swap Texts if you pasted the newer version on the left by mistake so additions and deletions point in the right direction.

Worked Example: Comparing "Our web tools process files on the server." against "Our free web tools process files locally in your browser." highlights free and locally in your browser as green additions and on the server as a red deletion.
