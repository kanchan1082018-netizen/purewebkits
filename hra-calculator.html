Complete User Guide to House Rent Allowance (HRA) Exemption Under Section 10(13A)

How HRA Exemption Works for Salaried Taxpayers Living in Rented Homes

For salaried employees who receive House Rent Allowance (HRA) as part of their compensation package and live in rented accommodation, Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act provides an exemption on the lowest of three statutory figures calculated over the relevant period. Any portion of HRA received above that exempt limit becomes taxable HRA (subject to your applicable income tax regime and slab rules—note that HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) applies when opting for the Old Tax Regime rather than the default New Tax Regime). Section 10(13A) HRA Exemption Formula

Let Salary = Basic Salary + DA (forming part of retirement benefits, typically taken as Basic + DA here), HRA be actual HRA received, Rent be actual rent paid, and c = 0.50 for metro cities or 0.40 for non-metro cities:

Exempt HRA = min( Actual HRA Received , Rent Paid − 0.10 × Salary , c × Salary )
Taxable HRA = max(0, Actual HRA Received − Exempt HRA)

Helpful Notes When Claiming HRA Exemption

  • No Exemption If You Do Not Pay Rent or Live in Your Own Home: Rent must actually be incurred on a rented residence; paying rent to a spouse generally does not qualify, while rent paid to parents may be considered if backed by a valid agreement, actual transfer, and landlord reporting where applicable.
  • Keep Rent Receipts & Landlord PAN Handy: Employers typically require rent receipts and the landlord's PAN when annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000 (₹8,333/month).
  • Check Regime Choice Before Filing: Compare tax liability across regimes with a tax advisor or payroll team before finalizing your declaration.

Worked Example: With monthly Basic Salary of ₹50,000 (₹6,00,000/yr), DA ₹0, HRA received ₹20,000/mo (₹2,40,000/yr), and rent paid ₹18,000/mo (₹2,16,000/yr) in a Metro city: (1) Actual HRA = ₹2,40,000, (2) Rent − 10% Salary = ₹2,16,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹1,56,000, and (3) 50% of Salary = ₹3,00,000. The minimum is ₹1,56,000/yr (₹13,000/mo) exempt, leaving ₹84,000/yr taxable.
