How Browser Text-to-Speech (TTS), Speech Synthesis & Speed Control Work

Why Listening to Written Text Boosts Proofreading, Learning & Accessibility

Text-to-Speech (TTS) technology converts written digital text into natural-sounding spoken audio using your operating system's native neural and phonetic voice engines via the W3C Web Speech API. Writers and editors rely on TTS proofreading because hearing your own articles, cover letters, or scripts read aloud immediately exposes awkward phrasing, missing words, and repetitive sentences that silent reading often misses. Students and researchers use adjustable playback speeds (1.2x to 1.5x) to absorb long study notes hands-free, while users with dyslexia or visual fatigue benefit from effortless audio narration without installing heavy software.

How to Convert Text to Audio in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Paste or Type Your Text: Enter any paragraph, blog draft, email, or study notes into the editor box above.
  2. Customize Voice, Speed & Pitch: Select your preferred English, Hindi, or international system voice, and adjust the speaking rate (0.5x to 2.0x) and pitch.
  3. Click Listen, Pause, or Stop: Press Listen / Speak to start immediate local narration, or use Pause and Stop anytime.

Spoken Narration Duration Formula & Worked Example

Average conversational English narration runs at approximately 150 words per minute (WPM) at standard 1.0x speed, scaling proportionally with your chosen playback rate multiplier:

Estimated Narration Time (Minutes) = Total Word Count / ( 150 WPM × Speed Rate )

Worked Example: Suppose you paste a 900-word blog article into the reader and set the speaking rate to 1.5x speed.

Effective narration pace = 150 WPM × 1.5 = 225 words per minute.

Total listening duration = 900 words / 225 WPM = 4.0 minutes (4 minutes 0 seconds)—saving 2 full minutes compared to standard 1.0x playback.
