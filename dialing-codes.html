Complete User Guide to International Dialing Codes, IDD Exit Prefixes & E.164 Numbers

How International Telephone Dialing Works Across Countries

Placing an international phone call or storing global contacts in a CRM, mobile phone, or payment system requires combining a destination country calling code (assigned under the ITU-T E.164 recommendation, such as +91 for India, +1 for the United States and Canada, +44 for the United Kingdom, or +971 for the UAE) with the local subscriber number—and dropping any domestic national trunk prefix (most commonly a leading 0). On mobile phones, dialing + automatically invokes the international access code; from a traditional landline, callers dial their own country's IDD (International Direct Dialing) exit prefix (such as 00 in India, the UK, and most of Europe, or 011 in North America) followed by the country code and subscriber number. International Dialing & E.164 Structure

Standard international phone numbers omit the domestic trunk zero and keep total digits within the 15-digit E.164 limit:

Mobile / Universal Format: +[Country Code][National Significant Number without leading 0]
Landline IDD Format: [Caller Country Exit Prefix (00 / 011)] + [Country Code] + [Subscriber Number]

Helpful Tips When Dialing Internationally

  • Drop the Leading Trunk Zero: For example, a UK mobile number written domestically as 07700 900123 is dialed internationally as +44 7700 900123 (without the leading 0).
  • Remember Shared Country Codes (+1): The United States, Canada, and several Caribbean nations share country code +1 under the North American Numbering Plan (NANP), differentiated by 3-digit area codes.
  • Use "+" When Saving Contacts: Storing numbers in + E.164 format ensures calls and messaging apps work both at home and while roaming abroad.

Worked Example: Looking up India returns dialing code +91 (ISO IN / IND, local IDD exit 00); entering local number 098765 43210 strips the leading 0 and formats the E.164 number as +919876543210.
