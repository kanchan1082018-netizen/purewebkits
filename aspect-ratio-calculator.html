How Aspect Ratio Reduction (GCD), Cross-Multiplication & CSS aspect-ratio Work

Why Preserving Exact Aspect Ratios Prevents Stretched Videos & Cropped Thumbnails

An image or video's aspect ratio expresses the proportional relationship between its width and height as two simplified whole numbers separated by a colon (such as 16:9 for widescreen HD video or 9:16 for vertical mobile Reels and Shorts). When designers scale a banner, YouTube thumbnail, or responsive web container without locking the original aspect ratio, faces appear horizontally stretched or vertically squashed—or social media platforms automatically crop away important headline text. By reducing raw pixel dimensions via their Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) and cross-multiplying target dimensions, you can resize any asset cleanly while keeping 100% of your composition intact.

GCD Aspect Ratio Simplification & Cross-Multiplication Formulas + Worked Example

To simplify any resolution W1 × H1 into its lowest whole-number ratio and solve for a new target height H2:

Simplified Ratio = (W1 / GCD) : (H1 / GCD)  |  H2 = round( W2 × H1 / W1 )

Worked Example: Suppose you have a 4K video frame measuring 3840 × 2160 px and want to scale it down to a 1280 px wide web thumbnail.

The Greatest Common Divisor of 3840 and 2160 is 240, simplifying the aspect ratio to (3840/240) : (2160/240) = 16 : 9.

Target Height H2 = (1280 × 2160) / 3840 = 720 px → Exact proportional resolution = 1280 × 720 px.
