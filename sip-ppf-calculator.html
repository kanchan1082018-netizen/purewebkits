Complete User Guide to Mutual Fund SIP and Public Provident Fund (PPF) Compounding

How SIP and PPF Help Build Long-Term Wealth Differently

Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) and the Public Provident Fund (PPF) are two of the most widely used long-term wealth-building vehicles in India, each serving a distinct role. A Mutual Fund SIP invests a fixed monthly amount into market-linked equity or hybrid funds—benefiting from rupee-cost averaging, optional annual step-ups, and long-term compounding, though returns fluctuate with market performance. By contrast, PPF is a government-backed, fixed-income savings scheme with a 15-year initial lock-in (extendable in 5-year blocks), an annual contribution cap of ₹1,50,000 per financial year, annual interest compounding, and exempt-exempt-exempt (EEE) tax treatment under applicable provisions. SIP & PPF Compounding Formulas

For standard SIP with monthly deposit P, monthly rate i = (R / 100) / 12, and n = 12 × Years months (or annual compounding A_yr for PPF):

SIP Future Value (Beginning-of-Month): FV = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) / i] × (1 + i)
PPF Annual Compounding: Balance_t = (Balance_{t−1} + Annual_Deposit) × (1 + r_ppf)

Helpful Tips for SIP and PPF Planning

  • Observe the ₹1,50,000 Annual PPF Deposit Ceiling: Contributions above ₹1,50,000 in a financial year do not earn PPF interest or tax deduction; depositing early in April maximizes interest across all 12 months.
  • Use Annual Step-Up in SIPs as Income Grows: Increasing your monthly SIP by even 5%–10% each year significantly boosts your 15- to 25-year corpus.
  • Account for Taxes & Market Risk: Equity/debt mutual fund gains are subject to prevailing capital gains tax rules upon redemption, and historical returns do not guarantee future performance.

Worked Example: Investing ₹10,000/month via SIP for 15 years at 12% p.a. (0% step-up) invests ₹18,00,000.00 and projects a maturity corpus of roughly ₹50,45,760.00 (₹32,45,760.00 wealth gained, or a 2.80× multiplier).
