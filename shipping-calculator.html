Complete User Guide to Global Shipping Costs, Volumetric Weight, and Chargeable Weight

Why Carriers Charge by Volumetric Weight When Parcels Are Bulky

In international air and express shipping, cargo space inside aircraft and delivery vehicles is limited by volume as well as weight. If a large box contains lightweight items—such as apparel, foam, packaging, or plush goods—it takes up valuable space even though its scale weight is low. To price space fairly, carriers calculate volumetric weight (also called dimensional weight) from the outer box dimensions and bill based on the chargeable weight—the greater of actual scale weight or volumetric weight—plus applicable fuel surcharges and fixed handling, insurance, or customs preparation fees. Volumetric Weight, Chargeable Weight & Total Cost Formula

Using box dimensions L × W × H (in cm), volumetric divisor D, actual weight W_act, rate per kg R, surcharge percentage S%, and fixed fee F:

Volumetric Weight (kg) = (L × W × H) / D  |  Chargeable Weight = max(W_act, Volumetric Weight)
Base Freight = Chargeable Weight × R  |  Total Cost = Base Freight × (1 + S% / 100) + F

Helpful Tips for Lowering International Shipping Costs

  • Downsize Outer Packaging Where Safe: Trimming excess box height or width lowers volumetric weight directly.
  • Compare Carrier Divisors Before Booking: A lower divisor (like 4000) produces a higher volumetric weight than 5000 or 6000 for the same box size.
  • Confirm Destination Duties & Taxes Separately: Import customs duties and destination VAT/GST depend on HS tariff codes, declared value, and destination country rules.

Worked Example: A 3 kg parcel in a 40 × 30 × 25 cm box has a volumetric weight of (40 × 30 × 25) / 5000 = 6.00 kg. Since 6.00 kg > 3 kg, chargeable weight is 6.00 kg. At $12.00/kg ($72.00 base freight) plus a 10% surcharge ($7.20) and $5.00 fee, total estimated shipping is $84.20.
