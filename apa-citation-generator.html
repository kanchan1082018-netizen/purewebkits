Complete User Guide to APA 7th Edition Citations, Author Formatting & In-Text Rules

Key Formatting Rules in APA Style (7th Edition)

APA Style (7th Edition), published by the American Psychological Association, is the standard citation framework across psychology, education, business, nursing, and the social sciences. Every APA citation pairs a concise in-text citation inside the body of your paper—either parenthetical, such as (Kahneman, 2024), or narrative, such as Kahneman (2024)—with a complete entry in your alphabetized References list at the end of the document. APA 7th introduced several practical simplifications over earlier editions: publisher locations (city/state) are no longer required for books, up to 20 authors are listed before using an ellipsis, in-text citations with 3 or more authors immediately shorten to the first author followed by et al., and DOIs are formatted as active https://doi.org/... links without "Retrieved from" before standard URLs unless content is designed to change over time. Standard APA 7th Edition Reference Templates

Notice how sentence case is applied to article/book titles while journal titles use title case and italics:

Website: Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of webpage. Site Name. https://url
Book: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of book: Subtitle (Edition). Publisher. https://doi.org/xxx
Journal: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of article. Journal Name, Volume(Issue), Pages. https://doi.org/xxx

Helpful Tips for Clean APA 7th Reference Lists

  • Use Sentence Case for Work Titles: Capitalize only the first word of a book, webpage, or article title, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns—keep journal titles in Title Case.
  • Include a Retrieval Date Only for Unarchived, Changing Pages: Static articles and blog posts do not need a retrieval date; reserve Retrieved Month Day, Year, from... for live dashboards or unarchived wiki pages.
  • Double-Check Hanging Indents & Alphabetical Order: In your word processor, format the References page double-spaced with a 0.5-inch hanging indent sorted alphabetically by the first author's surname.
