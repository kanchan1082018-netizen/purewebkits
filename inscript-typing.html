Complete User Guide to InScript Hindi Typing, Vowel Signs (Matras), and Halant Conjuncts

Why the InScript Layout Is the Standard for Indian Script Typing

The InScript (Indian Script) keyboard layout is the standard national layout defined for typing Indian languages—including Hindi in Unicode Devanagari—across operating systems and digital platforms. Unlike phonetic typing that guesses Latin spellings, InScript arranges vowels on the left side of the keyboard, consonants on the right, and shifts keys cleanly between unshifted and shifted positions so typists can build muscle memory for speed and accuracy. A browser-based InScript Hindi Typing Pad lets you type directly from a standard QWERTY keyboard or click on-screen keys—100% locally in your browser with zero server uploads.

How to Type Hindi With This InScript Pad in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Keep InScript Mapping Active (or Toggle Off): Leave Map Physical Keys (InScript) checked to type Devanagari characters directly on your physical keyboard, or uncheck it to type normally or paste text.
  2. Type Consonants, Matras & Shift Characters: Use unshifted keys for common consonants and vowel signs (matras), and hold Shift (or click Shift Layer on the virtual pad) for aspirated consonants, independent vowels, and conjunct modifiers.
  3. Copy or Export Your Text: Click Copy Hindi Text to copy to your clipboard or Download .txt to save a UTF-8 file locally.

How Consonants, Halant (्) & Matras Work in Unicode Devanagari

In Unicode Devanagari, each consonant carries an inherent अ vowel sound unless modified by a vowel sign (matra) or suppressed with the Halant (्):

Consonant + Matra (e.g., क + ा = का, क + ि = कि, क + ी = की, क + े = के)
Consonant 1 + Halant (्) + Consonant 2 = Conjunct (e.g., क् + ष = क्ष, त् + र = त्र, ज् + ञ = ज्ञ)

Helpful Tips for Smooth Devanagari Typing

  • Use Halant (्) for Half-Letters & Conjuncts: Typing a consonant followed by halant (d key in InScript) forms a half-consonant ready to join the next consonant.
  • Combine Virtual Keys With Your Physical Keyboard: Use the on-screen pad whenever you want a quick visual reminder of a shifted character or special mark.
  • Export as UTF-8 (.txt): Always use UTF-8 encoding when saving or sharing Devanagari text files.
