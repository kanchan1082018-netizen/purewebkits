How International SI Metric vs. US Customary Unit Conversions Work

Understanding Base-Unit Anchoring & Exact International Definitions

Across engineering, construction, international shipping, cooking, and software development, converting accurately between the International System of Units (SI Metric) and US Customary / Imperial units prevents costly specification errors. Since the 1959 International Yard and Pound Agreement, global standards bodies defined exact conversion constants anchored to SI base units: 1 inch = 25.4 millimeters exactly, 1 foot = 0.3048 meters, and 1 avoirdupois pound = 0.45359237 kilograms. Our bi-directional converter routes every linear measurement through its canonical SI base unit (meters, kilograms, square meters, liters, seconds, or bytes) and applies dedicated affine equations for non-zero-origin temperature scales (°C, °F, and K).

Linear Base-Factor & Temperature Conversion Formulas + Worked Example

Linear units convert by multiplying the input quantity by the ratio of unit base factors, while Celsius and Fahrenheit use an offset linear equation:

Linear: Output = Input × ( BaseFactorFrom / BaseFactorTo )  |  Temp: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32

Worked Example: Suppose an architect converts a room length of 12.5 meters into feet and inches.

Using the exact SI definition 1 foot = 0.3048 meters: 12.5 m / 0.3048 = 41.0105 feet.

Separating the decimal fraction (0.0105 ft × 12 = 0.13 inches) gives 41 feet, 0.13 inches (or 492.13 inches).
