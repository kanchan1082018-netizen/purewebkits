Complete User Guide to Margin Trading, Buying Power, Maintenance Margin & Margin Call Prices

How Initial Margin, Borrowed Funds, and Maintenance Margin Work

In equity and multi-asset margin trading, an investor posts a percentage of the total trade value—the Initial Margin Requirement (%)—as cash equity and borrows the remaining balance from a broker (Effective Leverage = 100 / Initial Margin %). For example, a 50% initial margin requirement provides 2.00x buying power on your deposited equity. If the market moves against your position, your account equity falls while the borrowed loan balance remains fixed (before margin interest). If your equity percentage drops below the broker's Maintenance Margin (%) threshold, the broker issues a margin call requiring additional cash or position reduction. Initial Margin & Maintenance Margin Call Formulas

Let V = Units × Entry be total position value, m_init = Init% / 100, and m_maint = Maint% / 100:

Required Equity = V × m_init  |  Borrowed Loan = V × (1 − m_init)
Long Margin Call Price = Entry × (1 − m_init) / (1 − m_maint)
Short Margin Call Price = Entry × (1 + m_init) / (1 + m_maint)

Helpful Tips for Managing Margin Risk

  • Keep a Cushion Above Maintenance Margin: Brokers may raise house maintenance requirements on volatile securities without advance notice, moving the margin call trigger closer to market price.
  • Account for Daily Margin Interest & Borrow Fees: Holding a leveraged or short position overnight incurs margin loan interest or stock borrow fees not included in raw price PnL.
  • Educational Simulation Only: This calculator models standard margin math and is not financial or investment advice.

Worked Example: Buying 100 shares at $100 ($10,000 position) with 50% initial margin requires $5,000.00 equity and $5,000.00 borrowed. With a 25% maintenance margin, a margin call triggers at $100 × (1 − 0.50) / (1 − 0.25) = $66.67, while reaching $112 produces +$1,200.00 PnL (+24.00% ROE).
