Complete User Guide to World Clocks, UTC Offsets, Daylight Saving Time, and Time Zone Conversion

Why Coordinating Across Time Zones Requires IANA Rules and DST Awareness

Working, traveling, or scheduling meetings across borders means accounting for differences in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) offsets as well as seasonal shifts caused by Daylight Saving Time (DST). Many countries and regions adjust their clocks forward or backward in spring and autumn—and not all regions shift on the same dates, while places such as India (UTC+5:30), Japan (UTC+9), and most of Arizona observe a fixed offset year-round. Using standard IANA time zone identifiers (such as America/New_York, Europe/London, Asia/Kolkata, and Asia/Tokyo) lets your browser apply historical and current DST rules automatically. A browser-based World Clock runs 100% locally in your browser using JavaScript's built-in Intl.DateTimeFormat engine—with zero server uploads or external clock APIs.

How UTC Offsets & Time Zone Conversion Work

Each time zone sits at an offset relative to UTC (including half-hour and 45-minute offsets such as UTC+5:30 in India or UTC+5:45 in Nepal):

Local Time = UTC Time + Zone Offset (including active DST adjustment for that date)
Time Difference Between Zones = Target Offset − Source Offset

Helpful Tips for Cross-Border Scheduling

  • Check Both Sides Around DST Transition Weeks: Spring-forward and fall-back shifts in North America and Europe happen on different Sundays, briefly changing the hour gap with fixed-offset regions.
  • Specify Time Zone Abbreviations & Date Clearly: Always include the city or time zone alongside the hour when inviting international participants so nobody misreads midnight or noon.
  • Use Convert Mode for Future Dates: Testing a future date in Convert Time Zone reflects the DST rule applicable on that specific calendar date.

Worked Example: When it is 12:00 PM (Noon) in London (UTC+0 in winter / BST UTC+1 in summer), India Standard Time (IST, UTC+5:30) is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead (5:30 PM in winter or 4:30 PM during BST), while New York (ET, UTC−5) is 5 hours behind (7:00 AM).
