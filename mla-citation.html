Complete User Guide to MLA 9th Edition Citations, Core Elements & In-Text Rules

How the MLA 9th Edition Core-Element System Works

MLA Style (9th Edition), published by the Modern Language Association, is the standard citation system for English literature, humanities, foreign languages, media studies, and cultural analysis. Unlike older editions that required rigid, separate formulas for dozens of medium types, MLA 9th organizes every Works Cited entry around a universal sequence of up to nine Core Elements separated by periods and commas: 1. Author. 2. Title of source. 3. Title of container, 4. Contributor, 5. Version, 6. Number, 7. Publisher, 8. Publication date, and 9. Location. Self-contained works like standalone books have italicized titles, whereas shorter works nested inside a larger container—such as a web article inside a website, a chapter inside an anthology, or an article inside a scholarly journal—place the source title in quotation marks and italicize the container title. In-text citations use a concise author–page format like (Orwell 42) without a comma or year. MLA 9th Edition Core-Element Templates

Notice that MLA 9th uses full first names (rather than initials), Title Case for all titles, and omits http:// or https:// from web URLs (while keeping https://doi.org/ for DOIs):

Website: Last, First. "Title of Webpage." Website Name, Publisher, Day Mon. Year, URL.
Book: Last, First. Title of Book. Edition, Publisher, Year.
Journal: Last, First. "Title of Article." Journal Name, vol. #, no. #, Year, pp. #-#. DOI/URL.

Helpful Tips for Formatting an MLA 9th Works Cited Page

  • Use "Author, First, et al." for Three or More Authors: Unlike APA 7th, which lists up to 20 authors in the reference list, MLA 9th shortens 3 or more authors to the first author's name followed by et al. in both the Works Cited entry and in-text citation.
  • Abbreviate Months Longer Than Four Letters: In MLA dates (Day Mon. Year), abbreviate Jan., Feb., Mar., Apr., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., and Dec., while spelling out May, June, and July in full.
  • Omit Publisher When Identical to Website Name: If a website's name and publisher are essentially the same (e.g., The New York Times), omit the redundant publisher element.

Worked Example: For book author Orwell, George, title 1984, publisher Signet Classic, and year 1950, MLA 9th formats the Works Cited entry as Orwell, George. 1984. Signet Classic, 1950. and the parenthetical in-text citation as (Orwell 42).
