Complete User Guide to Daily Calorie Intake, TDEE Adjustments, and Balanced Macros

How Daily Calorie Needs Connect Resting Metabolism, Movement, and Goals

Estimating your daily calorie intake starts with understanding how much energy your body uses at rest—your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)—and how much additional energy you spend through daily movement and exercise—captured by your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE). From that maintenance baseline, adjusting calories moderately up or down provides a structured starting point for weight maintenance, gradual weight loss, or lean weight gain. Pairing calorie targets with general macronutrient splits (protein, carbohydrates, and dietary fats) helps turn a single daily number into practical meal planning. All calculations run 100% client-side in your browser using JavaScript, keeping your personal measurements private on your device.

How to Estimate Your Daily Calorie Intake in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Enter Your Measurements & Units: Choose Metric (kg / cm) or Imperial (lb / in) and fill in sex constant, age, weight, and height, or click Load Sample Profile.
  2. Select Activity Level & Goal: Pick the activity multiplier matching your weekly routine and choose whether you want to maintain weight, aim for a moderate deficit (−20%), or aim for a moderate surplus (+15%).
  3. Review Targets & Export Summary: Check your daily target calories, maintenance TDEE, and sample 25%/45%/30% macro figures, then click Copy Calorie Plan or Download .txt.

Mifflin-St Jeor & Goal Intake Formulas

Using metric weight W (kg), height H (cm), age A (years), activity factor AF, and goal multiplier GM:

BMR = (10 × W) + (6.25 × H) − (5 × A) + Offset (+5 Male / −161 Female)
TDEE = BMR × AF  |  Target Intake = TDEE × GM

General Nutrition Context & Safe Habits

  • Aim for Gradual Adjustments Rather Than Extremes: Moderate shifts like −20% or +15% are easier to sustain than severe restrictions; consult a qualified healthcare provider or registered dietitian for individualized medical nutrition advice.
  • Observe Trends Over 2–3 Weeks: Formula estimates are starting points—track how your body responds over multiple weeks before making big changes.
  • Prioritize Whole Foods & Hydration: Balance protein, fiber-rich carbohydrates, healthy fats, and daily water intake alongside your calorie target.

Worked Example: A 28-year-old male (70 kg, 175 cm) with a resting BMR of roughly 1,674 kcal/day and a Lightly Active multiplier of 1.375 has an estimated maintenance TDEE of about 2,301 kcal/day.
