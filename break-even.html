Complete User Guide to Break-Even Point Analysis, Contribution Margin & Target Profit Planning

How Fixed Costs, Variable Costs, and Contribution Margin Determine Your Break-Even Point

In managerial accounting, pricing strategy, and startup financial modeling, your Break-Even Point (BEP) is the exact sales volume—measured both in units sold and in top-line revenue—where total revenue equals total costs (Fixed Costs + Total Variable Costs), resulting in zero net operating profit and zero loss. Every unit sold above your variable production/fulfillment cost contributes its Contribution Margin per Unit (Selling Price − Variable Cost) toward paying down fixed overhead such as rent, salaries, software subscriptions, and equipment leases. Once cumulative contribution margin covers your period fixed costs, every subsequent unit sold generates operating profit equal to its contribution margin. A browser-based Break-Even Point Calculator runs these cost-volume-profit (CVP) equations 100% locally in your browser—with zero server uploads.

Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) & Break-Even Formulas

Let FC be fixed costs, P be selling price per unit, VC be variable cost per unit, and TP be optional target profit:

Contribution Margin / Unit (CM) = P − VC  |  CM Ratio = CM / P
Break-Even Units = FC / CM  |  Break-Even Revenue = FC / CM Ratio  |  Target Units = (FC + TP) / CM

Helpful Tips for Pricing & Cost Structure Decisions

  • Selling Price Must Exceed Variable Cost Per Unit: If Selling Price ≤ Variable Cost, contribution margin is zero or negative and each additional sale increases losses rather than covering fixed overhead.
  • Test Small Price or Variable Cost Improvements: Raising unit price by 10% or trimming variable cost per unit expands contribution margin and lowers your break-even unit threshold faster than cutting minor fixed expenses.
  • Match Time Periods Across Inputs: If you enter monthly fixed costs, your break-even units and revenue represent monthly sales targets.

Worked Example: With $15,000 in monthly fixed costs, a $50 selling price, and $20 variable cost per unit, contribution margin is $30.00/unit (60.0% CM ratio). Breaking even requires $15,000 / $30 = 500 units ($25,000.00 in revenue), while earning $6,000 in operating profit requires ($15,000 + $6,000) / $30 = 700 units ($35,000.00).
