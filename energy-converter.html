Complete User Guide to Energy Unit Conversion, Joules, Calories, and Kilowatt-Hours

Why Converting Energy Units Accurately Matters Across Science, Nutrition, and Power

Energy appears in many domains using distinct units tailored to each field: physics and engineering use joules (J), kilojoules (kJ), megajoules (MJ), and electronvolts (eV); electricity billing and battery capacity use watt-hours (Wh) and kilowatt-hours (kWh); nutrition labels list kilocalories (kcal) (commonly called Calories); and heating or HVAC specs often reference British thermal units (BTU). Translating between these scales by hand can easily trip up powers of ten or conversion constants. A browser-based Energy Converter converts between all of these units through a unified SI joule reference while displaying every equivalent at once—100% locally in your browser with zero server uploads.

Canonical Joule Conversion Formula

Every supported unit converts through its exact joule equivalent (1 J = 1 kg·m²/s²):

Value in Target Unit = (Input Value × JoulesPerUnit[From]) / JoulesPerUnit[To]

Helpful Tips for Working With Energy Units

  • Distinguish Food Calories (kcal) From Gram Calories (cal): A nutrition label listing 200 Calories refers to 200 kilocalories (200 kcal), which equals 200,000 thermochemical gram calories.
  • Remember Energy vs. Power: Kilowatt-hours (kWh) measure total energy (power × time), whereas kilowatts (kW) measure rate of power.
  • Use Scientific Notation for Very Large or Small Values: Very small values like electronvolts or very large joule totals format cleanly in scientific notation when needed.

Worked Example: Converting 1 kilowatt-hour (1 kWh = 3,600,000 J) into kilojoules gives 3,600 kJ, which is also equal to 3.6 MJ or approximately 860.42 kcal.
