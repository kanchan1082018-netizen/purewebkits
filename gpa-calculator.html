Complete User Guide to Semester GPA, Cumulative CGPA & Weighted 4.0 / 5.0 Grade Scales

How Credit-Weighted Grade Point Average (GPA) Works

A Grade Point Average (GPA) is a credit-weighted mean of the letter grades earned across your courses rather than a simple unweighted average. Each letter grade maps to a numeric grade-point value on a standard 4.00 scale (for instance, A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, down to F = 0.0). Multiplying a course's grade-point value by its credit hours produces Quality Points (Quality Points = Grade Points × Credits), so a 4-credit lab science course influences your GPA twice as much as a 2-credit seminar. In high school or dual-enrollment programs using a Weighted GPA scale, Honors courses typically add +0.50 quality points and AP / IB / Dual-Enrollment courses add +1.00 quality point for passing grades (C− or above). Combining current semester quality points with prior cumulative credits and prior GPA also lets you project your exact Cumulative GPA (CGPA). Semester & Cumulative GPA Formulas

For courses i = 1 ... n with credit hours Ci and grade-point values Gi:

Semester GPA = Σ(Ci × Gi) / ΣCi
Cumulative GPA = (Prior GPA × Prior Credits + Semester Quality Points) / (Prior Credits + Semester Credits)

Helpful Tips for Academic & GPA Planning

  • Check Your Institution's Plus/Minus Scale: Most U.S. colleges use the standard 0.3 / 0.7 plus/minus scale (A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3), though a few universities treat A+ as 4.0 rather than 4.3—this calculator uses the standard A / A+ = 4.0 unweighted ceiling.
  • Exclude Pass/Fail (P/NP) Courses From Credit Totals: Non-graded Pass/Fail credits generally do not add to GPA denominator credits unless failed.
  • Protect High-Credit Courses First: Improving a 4-credit or 5-credit core class delivers the largest single-course boost to semester and cumulative GPA.

Worked Example: Earning an A (4.0) in a 4-credit Calculus class (16.0 pts), an A− (3.7) in a 4-credit Physics class (14.8 pts), a B+ (3.3) in a 3-credit History class (9.9 pts), and an A (4.0) in a 4-credit Computer Science class (16.0 pts) totals 56.70 quality points across 15 credits—yielding a 3.78 Semester GPA.
