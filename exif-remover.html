Complete User Guide to EXIF Metadata, GPS Privacy Risks & Stripping Image Metadata

Why Photos Carry Hidden EXIF, GPS & Device Metadata

Digital cameras and smartphones embed Exchangeable Image File Format (EXIF) metadata inside image files—recording details such as camera/phone make and model, capture timestamp, exposure settings, orientation, software version, and sometimes exact GPS latitude, longitude, and altitude where the photo was taken. While helpful for photography organization, sharing raw photos publicly on forums, marketplaces, or cloud links can unintentionally reveal your home or workplace location or device details. Re-rendering the decoded pixel buffer onto a fresh HTML5 and exporting a clean JPEG, PNG, or WebP file constructs a brand-new image stream without copying camera EXIF/APP1 or XMP metadata blocks. A browser-based EXIF Data Remover inspects and strips metadata 100% locally in your browser—with zero server uploads.

GPS Decimal Degree Conversion From EXIF DMS

When EXIF GPS tags store Degrees, Minutes, and Seconds (DMS), coordinates convert to decimal degrees as:

Decimal Degrees = Degrees + (Minutes / 60) + (Seconds / 3600)
Negative sign applied for South (lat) or West (lon) hemisphere

Helpful Tips for Photo Privacy & Archiving

  • Keep a Backup of Originals If You Need Camera EXIF Data: Stripping metadata is permanent on the downloaded file—keep your original camera files in a private archive if you later want timestamps or camera settings.
  • Choose PNG for Sharp Graphics or Screenshots: Lossless PNG avoids recompression artifacts on text, UI screenshots, and diagrams, while JPEG/WebP offer smaller file sizes for photos.
  • Note on Browser Canvas Export: Canvas re-encoding strips EXIF/APP1 metadata blocks; for archival workflows requiring ICC color-profile preservation, verify color appearance on your target display.
