Complete User Guide to MAC Addresses, OUI Vendor Prefixes, U/L Bits & IPv6 EUI-64

How 48-Bit MAC Addresses and IEEE OUI Prefixes Work

A Media Access Control (MAC) address is a 48-bit (6-byte / 12-hexadecimal-digit) hardware identifier assigned to network interfaces on Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth devices. The first three octets (24 bits) form the Organizationally Unique Identifier (OUI)—registered through the IEEE Registration Authority to identify the hardware manufacturer or vendor—while the remaining three octets (24 bits) are assigned by the vendor to uniquely identify each interface. Beyond vendor lookup, the two least significant bits of the first byte carry critical networking flags: Bit 0 (I/G — Individual/Group) distinguishes a Unicast address (0) from a Multicast/Broadcast address (1), and Bit 1 (U/L — Universal/Local) indicates whether the address is Universally Administered (UAA) by the vendor (0) or Locally Administered (LAA) by software/OS privacy MAC randomization (1, seen whenever the second hex digit has bit 2 set—i.e., ends in 2, 6, A, or E). Locally Administered Bit & Modified EUI-64 Conversion Formula

To check whether a MAC address is locally administered (private/randomized) and derive its modified IPv6 EUI-64:

Is Locally Administered = ((First_Byte & 0x02) !== 0)  |  Is Multicast = ((First_Byte & 0x01) !== 0)
Modified EUI-64 = [Byte0 ^ 0x02] : Byte1 : Byte2 : FF : FE : Byte3 : Byte4 : Byte5

Helpful Tips for Network Troubleshooting & Wi-Fi Privacy

  • Why Modern Phones & Laptops Show "Private" or "Randomized" MACs: iOS, Android, Windows, and macOS frequently generate locally administered MAC addresses (where the second hex digit is 2, 6, A, or E) per Wi-Fi network to prevent cross-network tracking—which means that specific randomized MAC won't match a factory OUI vendor table.
  • Broadcast & Multicast MACs Are Not Hardware Vendors: FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF is the Layer-2 broadcast address, and any address with an odd first byte (such as 01:00:5E:... for IPv4 multicast) is a group/multicast address rather than a single device.
  • Convert Between Formats Easily: Linux/macOS/Windows often display colon or hyphen notation, while Cisco IOS uses three groups of four hex digits separated by dots.

Worked Example: Taking MAC 3C:5A:B4:12:34:56 gives OUI 3C:5A:B4 (Google, Inc.), first byte 0x3C (binary 00111100 → Bit 0 = 0 Unicast, Bit 1 = 0 Universally Administered), and modified IPv6 EUI-64 3e5a:b4ff:fe12:3456.
