Complete User Guide to Dead Pixel Testing, Stuck Sub-Pixels & Panel Uniformity Checks

Dead Pixels vs. Stuck Sub-Pixels vs. Hot Pixels Explained

Every LCD, IPS, VA, or OLED display panel is made of millions of microscopic pixels, and each pixel typically contains three colored sub-pixels—Red, Green, and Blue—that blend together to form full color. A true dead pixel has failed completely and stays permanently unlit (appearing as a tiny black dot on bright backgrounds like Pure White or Light Gray), whereas a stuck sub-pixel remains permanently powered on in Red, Green, or Blue (showing as a bright red, green, or cyan/magenta/yellow pinpoint on a Pure Black screen). A hot pixel stays permanently bright white across all frames. Testing against uniform full-screen color fields—White, Black, Red, Green, Blue, 50% Gray, and a Pixel Grid—exposes isolated sub-pixel defects as well as IPS glow, clouding, or uneven panel uniformity that everyday windowed apps easily hide. Total Pixel & Sub-Pixel Count Formula

For horizontal resolution W and vertical resolution H (scaled by device pixel ratio DPR):

Total Pixels = W × H  |  Total RGB Sub-Pixels = 3 × W × H
1080p Full HD = 1920 × 1080 = 2,073,600 Pixels (6,220,800 Sub-Pixels)

Helpful Tips Before Filing a Monitor Warranty or Exchange Claim

  • Wipe Dust & Smudges First: Dried droplets, dust specks, or skin oils on a matte anti-glare coating frequently mimic dead or stuck pixels—gently clean the screen with a dry microfiber cloth before testing.
  • Check Manufacturer Dead-Pixel Policies: Many display makers specify a minimum count of bright or dark defects (often 1 to 5+ depending on panel grade and sub-pixel type) before qualifying for warranty replacement.
  • Use Auto-Cycle (3s) Gently: Rapid flashing colors can bother photosensitive viewers—step through manually with arrow keys if preferred.

Worked Example: On a 1920×1080 display (2,073,600 pixels), a pinpoint that looks bright green against a Pure Black (#000000) screen indicates a stuck green sub-pixel, whereas a black dot visible against Pure White (#FFFFFF) indicates a dead pixel.
