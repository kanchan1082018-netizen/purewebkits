Complete User Guide to Personal Net Worth, Assets, Liabilities, and Balance Sheet Tracking

Why Tracking Your Net Worth Gives a Clearer Financial Picture Than Income Alone

Income shows how much money flows in each month, whereas net worth measures your overall financial position at a point in time—the difference between everything you own (assets) and everything you owe (liabilities). Reviewing your personal balance sheet quarterly or annually shows whether paying down debt and building savings or investments are steadily increasing your equity over time. Because personal asset and debt figures are sensitive, a browser-based Net Worth Calculator computes all totals and ratios 100% locally in your browser using JavaScript—meaning none of your numbers are ever uploaded to a server.

How to Calculate Your Net Worth in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Enter Your Assets: Fill in current balances or realistic market values for Cash & Savings, Investments & Retirement, Real Estate / Home Value, Vehicles & Personal Property, and Other Assets.
  2. Enter Your Liabilities: Enter remaining balances for Mortgage Balance, Auto Loans, Student / Personal Loans, Credit Card Balances, and Other Debts.
  3. Inspect Ratios & Export Snapshot: Review your net worth, liquid assets, and debt-to-asset ratio, then click Copy Net Worth Summary or Download .txt to save a dated snapshot.

Net Worth & Debt-to-Asset Ratio Formulas

Across all asset items Aᵢ and liability items Lᵢ:

Total Assets = Σ Aᵢ  |  Total Liabilities = Σ Lᵢ
Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities  |  Debt-to-Asset Ratio = (Total Liabilities / Total Assets) × 100

Helpful Tips for Accurate Personal Balance Sheets

  • Use Conservative Resale Values for Homes & Vehicles: Estimating what an asset would realistically sell for today avoids inflating net worth.
  • Distinguish Liquid Assets From Illiquid Equity: Cash and brokerage accounts can be accessed faster than home equity or vehicles.
  • Track Progress Over Time Rather Than Comparing to Others: A negative net worth early in a career or after taking on a mortgage or student loan can still improve steadily year over year.

Worked Example: With $445,000.00 in total assets and $235,000.00 in total liabilities, net worth is $445,000.00 − $235,000.00 = $210,000.00, with $130,000.00 in liquid assets and a 52.8% debt-to-asset ratio.
