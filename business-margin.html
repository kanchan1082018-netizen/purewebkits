Complete User Guide to Business Margin, Markup Percentage & Target Pricing

How Gross Margin and Markup Differ (And Why Mixing Them Hurts Pricing)

Two of the most common pricing metrics in business—gross profit margin and markup—measure the exact same dollar profit from different denominators. Gross margin expresses profit as a percentage of the selling price (revenue), showing how much of each sales dollar stays in the business after direct cost of goods or delivery; markup expresses profit as a percentage of the cost, showing how much was added on top of cost to set the price. Because revenue is larger than cost whenever profit is positive, a 40% gross margin equals a 66.67% markup—and conversely, adding a 40% markup on cost yields a 28.57% gross margin, not 40%. Confusing the two can easily underprice products or services. Gross Margin, Markup & Target Price Formulas

For cost C, selling price P, profit Profit = P − C, and target margin decimal m = margin% / 100:

Gross Margin (%) = ((P − C) / P) × 100  |  Markup (%) = ((P − C) / C) × 100
Target Price (P) = C / (1 − m)  (for m < 1)

Helpful Tips for Healthy Unit Economics

  • Separate Gross Margin From Net Profit Margin: Gross margin accounts for direct cost of goods/service delivery—remember to cover fixed overhead, marketing, and taxes out of remaining profit.
  • Check Discount Impact Before Running Promotions: A small price discount trims profit dollars directly—test reduced prices in the calculator to see how margin compresses.
  • Never Set Target Margin at or Above 100%: A gross margin of 100% would imply zero cost; target margins for sustainable pricing sit below 100%.

Worked Example: With a direct cost of $60.00 and a selling price of $100.00, gross profit is $40.00, gross margin is $40.00 / $100.00 = 40.00%, and markup on cost is $40.00 / $60.00 = 66.67%.
