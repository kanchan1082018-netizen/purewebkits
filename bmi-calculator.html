How Body Mass Index (BMI), Ideal Weight Range & Health Categories Work

What Is Body Mass Index (BMI) and How Do Doctors Use It?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a globally standardized screening metric developed by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet and adopted by the World Health Organization (WHO) to evaluate whether an adult's body weight is proportionate to their height. Because BMI relies purely on weight and height without requiring specialized body-fat calipers or DEXA scans, clinics, fitness coaches, and life-insurance underwriters use it as a fast baseline indicator to screen for underweight malnutrition, overweight status, and elevated cardiometabolic risk. While BMI does not directly distinguish between lean muscle mass and visceral body fat (meaning heavily muscled athletes may register an elevated BMI despite low body fat), pairing BMI with waist circumference offers a reliable snapshot of overall metabolic health.

Metric & Imperial BMI Mathematical Formulas + Worked Example

BMI divides body weight in kilograms by the square of height in meters (with a 703 conversion factor when using pounds and inches):

Metric: BMI = Weight (kg) / [ Height (m) ]²  |  Imperial: BMI = 703 × Weight (lbs) / [ Height (in) ]²

Worked Example: Suppose an adult weighs 68 kg and measures 172 cm (1.72 meters) tall.

Height squared = 1.72 × 1.72 = 2.9584 m².

Body Mass Index = 68 / 2.9584 = 22.99 ≈ 23.0 kg/m², which sits squarely inside the Normal Weight (18.5 – 24.9) range (healthy weight for 172 cm: 54.7 kg to 73.7 kg).
