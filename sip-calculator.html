How Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) Compounding & Annual Step-Up Work

Why Regular SIP Investing + Rupee Cost Averaging Builds Long-Term Wealth

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows investors to contribute a fixed amount every month into equity index funds, mutual funds, or ETFs rather than trying to time market highs and lows. When market prices dip, your fixed monthly contribution automatically purchases more fund units; when prices rally, your existing units compound in value—a disciplined mechanism known as Rupee Cost Averaging (or Dollar Cost Averaging). Even more powerful is an Annual Step-Up SIP: increasing your monthly SIP by just 10% every year in line with salary increments can nearly double your final retirement corpus compared to a static monthly SIP.

How to Calculate Mutual Fund SIP Returns in 3 Easy Steps

  1. Select Monthly SIP or Lumpsum: Choose Monthly SIP for recurring monthly contributions or Lumpsum for a single one-time deposit.
  2. Adjust Amount, Return Rate & Years: Enter your investment amount, expected annual return (12% is a common benchmark for diversified equity funds), and tenure in years.
  3. Optional Annual Step-Up & Schedule: Slide Annual SIP Step-Up % to model yearly top-ups and click View Year-by-Year Table to inspect annual compounding milestones.

Standard SIP Future Value Formula + Worked Example

Monthly SIP maturity value compounds each monthly installment at the periodic monthly rate r = Annual Rate / 12 / 100 over n = Years × 12 months:

FV = P × [ ( (1 + r)n − 1 ) / r ] × (1 + r)  |  Lumpsum: FV = P × (1 + R)t

Worked Example: Suppose you invest ₹10,000 per month for 15 years (180 months) at an expected return of 12% p.a. (r = 0.01 per month).

Total Principal Invested = ₹10,000 × 180 = ₹18,00,000.

Applying the compound SIP formula produces a Future Maturity Value of ₹50,45,760—meaning ₹32,45,760 (64.3%) comes purely from compounding gains.
