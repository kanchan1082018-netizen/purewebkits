Complete User Guide to Billable Hours, Time Rounding Increments & Client Invoicing

How Time Rounding Increments Work in Freelancing and Consulting

Freelancers, consultants, agencies, and legal or technical contractors often track time in hours and minutes, then convert that raw duration into billable hours using a standard rounding increment—such as 15-minute (0.25 hour) increments, 6-minute (0.10 hour) increments, 30-minute (0.50 hour) increments, or exact minute billing. Rounding up to the nearest increment ensures short bursts of focused work are accounted for cleanly on client timesheets and invoices, while viewing the rounding delta helps you keep billing transparent and consistent with your client agreement. A browser-based Billable Hours Calculator computes billable hours, subtotal fees, tax, and invoice totals 100% locally in your browser using JavaScript—with zero server uploads.

Billable Hours & Invoice Formula

Let M_raw = Hours × 60 + Minutes be total raw minutes and S be the rounding step in minutes:

Rounded Minutes = ceil(M_raw / S) × S  |  Billable Hours = Rounded Minutes / 60
Subtotal = Billable Hours × Hourly Rate  |  Total Invoice = Subtotal × (1 + Tax% / 100)

Helpful Tips for Clean Client Timesheets & Invoices

  • Align Rounding Rules With Your Statement of Work (SOW): Confirm whether your client expects 15-minute, 6-minute, or exact-minute rounding before sending invoices.
  • Keep Additional Minutes Between 0 and 59: Enter full hours in Hours Worked and remaining minutes (0–59) in Additional Minutes.
  • Add Tax Only When Applicable: Leave Tax / GST / VAT (%) at 0 for untaxed quotes or enter your applicable tax rate.

Worked Example: Working 12 hours 20 minutes (740 raw minutes) at $85/hour with a 15-minute rounding increment rounds up to 750 minutes (12.50 billable hours), producing a subtotal and total invoice of $1,062.50 (at 0% tax).
