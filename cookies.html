1. What Are Cookies & Browser Storage Technologies?

Cookies are small text files placed on your computer, smartphone, or tablet by websites you visit. Alongside cookies, modern web browsers also provide localStorage and sessionStorage, which allow web applications to store small amounts of non-sensitive data locally on your device.

At PureWebKits, our browser utility tools process your inputs, images, text, and calculations directly on your device in client-side JavaScript—without uploading your private files to our servers. Cookies and similar technologies on this site are used for essential functionality, performance measurement, and supporting free access through advertising.

AdSense Transparency Note: PureWebKits offers free online tools supported in part by display advertising such as Google AdSense. Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies or similar identifiers to serve ads based on a user's prior visits to this website and/or other websites.

2. Types of Cookies & Storage We Use

A. Strictly Necessary & Functional Storage

These mechanisms help the site operate smoothly—such as remembering UI preferences, active filter states, or modal/accordion behavior during your session. Without them, certain interface conveniences may reset when you reload or navigate between pages.

B. Analytics & Performance Cookies

Analytics tools help us understand aggregate traffic patterns—such as which utility pages are most helpful, how fast pages load across devices, and where users encounter errors—so we can keep improving tool reliability and user experience.

C. Advertising Cookies (Google AdSense & Partners)

When advertising is enabled via Google AdSense or authorized ad partners, cookies may be used to:

  • Deliver relevant advertisements (contextual or interest-based/personalized ads where permitted by your region and consent settings).
  • Measure ad impressions, clicks, and conversion/engagement effectiveness.
  • Limit how often you see the same ad (frequency capping) and help detect invalid traffic or click fraud.

Users in applicable regions (such as the EEA, UK, or Switzerland, where consent frameworks apply) may be presented with a consent choice for personalized advertising; even if personalized ads are declined or disabled, non-personalized or contextual ads may still appear.

3. How You Can Manage or Disable Cookies

You have full control over cookies in your browser and can inspect, block, or delete them at any time:

  1. Browser Cookie Settings: Open your browser's Privacy or Security settings (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, or mobile browsers) to block third-party cookies, clear existing cookies, or browse in Incognito/Private mode.
  2. Google Ad Settings & Opt-Out Tools: You can review or customize your Google ad personalization preferences through Google's My Ad Center / Ad Settings and learn more about how Google uses data from partner sites on Google's Advertising & Privacy documentation pages.
  3. Industry Opt-Out Options: Depending on your country, industry tools such as the Network Advertising Initiative (NAI) or Digital Advertising Alliance (DAA) opt-out pages also provide guidance on managing interest-based ad preferences.

Please note that blocking or deleting cookies may affect ad relevance or reset certain site preferences, though your local client-side tools will continue to work in your browser.
