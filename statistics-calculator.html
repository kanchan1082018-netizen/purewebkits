Complete User Guide to Descriptive Statistics, Central Tendency, and Spread

Why Descriptive Statistics Help Summarize Data Clearly and Accurately

Raw lists of numbers—whether test scores, response times, sales figures, survey ratings, or lab measurements—are hard to interpret at a glance without summary metrics. Descriptive statistics summarize a dataset along two complementary dimensions: central tendency (mean, median, and mode, which describe typical values) and dispersion or spread (minimum, maximum, range, variance, and standard deviation, which describe how widely values vary). Mean, Variance & Standard Deviation Formulas

For n observations x₁, x₂, ..., xₛ with arithmetic mean μ (or sample mean x̄):

Mean = (Σ xᵢ) / n  |  Population Variance (σ²) = [Σ(xᵢ − μ)²] / n
Sample Variance (s²) = [Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²] / (n − 1)   (for n > 1)

Choosing Between Mean vs. Median and Sample vs. Population

  • Use Median When Outliers Are Present: Extreme high or low values pull the mean toward them, whereas the middle value of sorted data (the median) stays resistant to skew.
  • Use Sample (n − 1) for a Subset of Data: When your numbers represent a sample drawn from a larger population, dividing by n − 1 (Bessel's correction) provides an unbiased variance estimate.
  • Use Population (n) When Measuring the Entire Group: When your numbers cover every member of the defined population, divide by n.

Worked Example: For the dataset 12, 15, 18, 20, 25 (n = 5, sum = 90), the Mean is 18, the Median is 18, the Range is 25 − 12 = 13, and the Sample Standard Deviation is approximately 4.95.
